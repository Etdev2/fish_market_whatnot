# Whatnot policy on selling raw seafood

Research for [issue #2](https://github.com/Etdev2/fish_market_whatnot/issues/2).
**All Whatnot pages checked 2026-08-11.**

> Not legal advice. Platform policies change frequently — Whatnot's own Food &
> Drink Policy was last edited 2026-06-18 and the fee page was edited the same
> morning this was checked. Re-verify before relying on any of it.

## Summary

- **NO KILL SHOT. The answer is YES-BUT.** Whatnot explicitly permits the sale of
  fresh, perishable raw seafood, shipped to consumers. It has built a dedicated
  product category for it: **Fresh & Specialty Food**, covering "fresh meat,
  seafood, produce, dairy, and prepared meals." The platform does not forbid the
  product category, so the effort survives this gate and moves on to the
  California licensing questions.
- **It is a gated category — approval is required before you can sell.** You
  apply by scheduling a show or listing a product in the category; Whatnot
  reviews and replies within 5–7 business days, evaluating "seller experience,
  compliance requirements and overall alignment with marketplace standards."
  This is a discretionary approval, not a checkbox.
- **Biggest product-scope constraint: raw molluscan shellfish (oysters, clams,
  mussels, whole scallops) requires the seller to be listed on the Interstate
  Certified Shellfish Shippers List (ICSSL).** That is a hard, verifiable federal
  bar. Finfish (the likely California catch — rockfish, halibut, tuna, sea bass)
  carries no equivalent named-list requirement in Whatnot's policy, only general
  "comply with FDA/state law, HACCP as applicable."
- **Cold-chain rules are strict and specific.** Fresh/live seafood must ship
  **1-day**; frozen seafood may ship 1- or 2-day. **No temperature-controlled
  shipping on Fridays.** Coolants must cover transit time **plus a 24-hour
  buffer**. Spoilage complaints can trigger billbacks and loss of category access.
- **US-only.** Whatnot permits perishable food sales **only within the United
  States**. There is no interstate (state-to-state) restriction of Whatnot's own
  — it defers to applicable law — but the international market is closed.
- **Fees: 8% commission + 2.9% of total order value + $0.30.** Fresh & Specialty
  Food falls under "All other categories." No food-specific fee premium found.
- **Whatnot's written policy does not distinguish licensed food business from
  individual seller.** Same gate, same rules for both. The distinction is
  effectively imported from the underlying law Whatnot tells you to comply with.

## Findings by sub-question

### 1. Prohibited/restricted items policy as applied to food and raw seafood

The master [Prohibited or Restricted Items](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/360061224972-Prohibited-or-Restricted-Items-on-Whatnot)
page (updated 2026-08-11) does **not** ban food. It contains only a pointer:

> **Food & drink policy** — Whatnot has specific rules around the sale of food
> and drink items. Please see Whatnot's Food & Drink Policy for more details.

The operative document is the [Whatnot Food & Drink Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/27681555378701-Whatnot-Food-Drink-Policy)
(created 2024-06-18, last edited 2026-06-18). It opens:

> To ensure the safety of our community and the quality of items sold on Whatnot,
> we have rules and restrictions when selling food and drink. To sell fresh,
> perishable food items, we require sellers to be approved to sell in the Fresh &
> Specialty Food Category.

**Seafood is affirmatively in scope, not excluded.** The category is defined as:

> we've created the Fresh & Specialty Food category for fresh meat, seafood,
> produce, dairy, and prepared meals. **All perishable food on Whatnot must be
> sold in the Fresh & Specialty Food Category.**

And the FAQ definition of perishable explicitly names it:

> Fresh or perishable food includes items that are not shelf stable, which
> includes: **raw or cooked meat, poultry, and seafood** […] frozen foods

**The prohibited list** (none of which categorically excludes California finfish):

> - Alcoholic beverages
> - Expired or recalled items. Buyers must receive their items prior to any
>   expiration date or best before date.
> - Unsafe food and drink items or those that do not comply with applicable laws
>   or regulations, including: uninspected meat or poultry; wild or hunted game
>   meats (e.g., venison, boar, elk); unpasteurized dairy products or juice
>   (excluding cheese aged at least 60 days…); food or drink containing
>   controlled substances or illegal ingredients; food or drink that Whatnot
>   determines, at its sole discretion, to be unsafe, non-compliant, misleading,
>   or otherwise unsuitable for the platform
> - Food or drink that violates any applicable laws, including food safety laws
>   or regulations, including restrictions on the shipment of fresh produce to
>   and from certain jurisdictions
> - Food or drink lacking required labeling, including ingredient lists, and
>   allergen disclosures

Note carefully: the "wild or hunted game meats" ban lists **land mammals**
(venison, boar, elk) and sits under the meat/poultry inspection heading.
Wild-caught fish is not named there, and the separate Seafood clause below
contemplates wild-caught product. **Do not read "wild game" as banning wild-caught
fish** — but this is an inference from structure, not an explicit carve-out, and
is worth a support ticket to confirm (see Open Questions).

**The two product-specific clauses:**

> **Raw Meat, Raw Poultry:** Raw meat or raw poultry must be USDA-inspected, with
> a USDA inspection seal. We prohibit the sale of uninspected, state-inspected-only,
> or home-processed meat.

> **Seafood:** To sell raw molluscan shellfish (i.e., oysters, clams, mussels, and
> whole scallops) sellers must be listed on the Interstate Certified Shellfish
> Shippers List (ICSSL) and those foods must comply with National Shellfish
> Sanitation Program (NSSP) guidelines.

This is the single most important line for product scoping. Whatnot imposes a
**named federal list requirement on molluscan shellfish only**. It imposes **no
equivalent named certification on finfish**. Finfish falls back to the general
seller requirements below.

Also relevant: the [Animal Related Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/30332489173261-Animal-Related-Policy)
(updated 2026-08-11) permits live aquatic sales and bans protected species:

> **Live animals:** Live animals are not permitted to be offered for sale… in most
> cases. Whatnot permits the sale of **fish, aquatic snails, coral, lobster, and
> live bait and worms.**

> **Endangered and regulated animal items:** Animal items derived from endangered
> or threatened species, or that are otherwise protected by law, are not permitted…
> including items derived from: endangered or threatened animals as identified by
> the CITES Appendix I […] animals otherwise prohibited or protected by applicable laws

Practical read: species selection matters. Anything CITES Appendix I or otherwise
legally protected is off. Ordinary California commercial finfish is not.

### 2. Seller verification, category approval, special application

**Three layers, all mandatory.**

**Layer 1 — baseline seller verification.** Per [Why Whatnot Requires Seller
Verification](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/38855359354381-Why-Whatnot-Requires-Seller-Verification)
(updated 2026-08-11):

> all sellers are required to verify their phone number, payment method, and
> government-issued ID

ID verification runs through Stripe or Persona for KYC. Account holders must be 18+.

**Layer 2 — gated category approval.** Per [How to sell in Gated Categories](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/19527461974541-How-to-sell-in-Gated-Categories-on-Whatnot)
(updated 2026-08-09), Fresh & Specialty Foods is one of five gated categories
(alongside Luxury Bags & Accessories, two Surprise Sets categories, and Supplements):

> Selling Fresh & Specialty Foods requires experience and expertise, which is why
> we require sellers to apply and receive approval to sell in this category. To
> apply, schedule a show or list a product in the Fresh & Specialty Foods category
> and follow the application process prompts.

The Food & Drink Policy sets the SLA and the discretionary standard:

> Whatnot will review your application and reply within **5-7 business days**.

> Applications will be evaluated based on a combination of factors, including
> **seller experience, compliance requirements and overall alignment with
> marketplace standards.**

Category access can be revoked; appeals go by replying to the notification email,
and questions go to trustandsafety@whatnot.com. Buyers can see a seller's gated
category permissions via the Report flow ("Seller's Category Permissions").

**Layer 3 — ongoing documentation on demand.** From the Food & Drink Policy:

> **Request for further information:** Sellers will provide Whatnot with applicable
> compliance documentation or evidence upon request.

So licensing paperwork is not necessarily demanded up front, but Whatnot reserves
the right to demand proof at any time. Operating without the California paperwork
in hand is a standing account risk even if the application clears.

### 3. Whatnot's own shipping-window, cold-chain, and packaging requirements

Yes — extensive, and stricter than typical marketplace rules.

**Shipping windows (the Friday rule):**

> **When to ship: end-of-week shipping restrictions.** To avoid weekend transit
> delays, sellers **must not ship temperature-controlled food on Fridays**. If
> shipping on a Thursday, sellers must use 1-day shipping, or wait until the
> following Monday if using 2-day shipping. This does not apply if the seller's
> carrier confirms Saturday or Sunday delivery.

This materially constrains show scheduling — a Friday-night show cannot ship until
Monday unless weekend delivery is confirmed.

**Required shipping speed by product type** (verbatim table):

| Product | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Fresh and live seafood (not frozen)** — incl. fresh and live lobster, oysters, crabs, scallops, shrimp | **1-day shipping required** |
| Refrigerated food — prepared meals, fresh meat, dairy, cheese, deli meat, refrigerated desserts/pastries | 1 or 2-day shipping required |
| Frozen — frozen meat & seafood, frozen meals, frozen desserts | 1 or 2-day shipping required |

**Fresh (never-frozen) seafood is the single most expensive shipping tier on the
platform: 1-day, mandatory.** Selling the product frozen unlocks the cheaper 2-day
option. That is a live business-model lever, not a compliance detail.

**Cold-chain and packaging requirements** (all mandatory "must" language):

> sellers must take into account their shipping method and include enough coolants
> to cover **transit time plus additional 24-hour buffer period** in case of delays

> **Insulation:** Sellers must use appropriate insulation to maintain the required
> temperature range as needed (e.g., EPS [expanded polystyrene] foam coolers,
> insulated corrugated boxes).

> **Frozen products:** Dry ice or gel packs rated for sub-zero temperatures… ensure
> you comply with carrier requirements, in particular sellers **must not use more
> than 5.5 pounds of dry ice** when shipping their items via air.

> **Refrigerated products:** Gel packs frozen at time of packing sufficient to
> maintain a safe temperature for the full transit window plus a 24-hour buffer.

Plus: secure packing against movement/leakage, and packages "should" be clearly
marked perishable and temperature-sensitive.

**Buyer-paid flat-rate shipping** from [Fresh & Specialty Foods Best Practices](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/42924449850509-Fresh-Specialty-Foods-on-Whatnot-Best-Practices)
(updated 2026-07-15). Buyers pay shipping; rates are flat and tiered by weight:

| Shipping method | 0–7 lb | 7–20 lb | 20–50 lb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UPS Second Day Air | $25 | $35 | $45 |
| UPS Next Day Air Saver | $35 | $45 | $55 |
| UPS Next Day Air | $45 | $55 | $65 |

This is a major unit-economics input: **a fresh-seafood buyer pays $35–$65 in
shipping** on top of the fish. Frozen product at 2-day drops that to $25–$45.

Also from Best Practices: a **packaging weight buffer** (default 2.5 lb, seller-
adjustable) is added once per shipment to cover box/coolant weight — list product
weight only. Consistently under-setting it can get you charged the difference.
A seller picks Next Day Air *or* Second Day Air per show, not both, though
non-express USPS can run alongside for non-food items in the same show.

**Consequences of spoilage:**

> If we receive spoilage complaints, we may request information about your
> packaging and shipping practices, **hold your responsible for refunds (i.e.,
> billbacks)**, and/or take action against your account (e.g., removing your
> access to the Fresh & Specialty Foods category or all seller permissions)
> depending on the severity or recurrence of issues.

Buyers are told not to ship to P.O. Boxes or APO/FPO addresses.

### 4. Restrictions on shipping perishables across state lines

**Whatnot imposes a country-level restriction, not a state-level one.**

> **US-only:** To maintain a quality experience, we currently only permit the sale
> of perishable food within the United States.

Interstate shipping within the US is therefore permitted by the platform. Whatnot
adds no state-pair blocklist of its own for seafood. It does, however, push the
legal burden onto the seller in two places:

> Food or drink that violates any applicable laws, including food safety laws or
> regulations, **including restrictions on the shipment of fresh produce to and
> from certain jurisdictions** [prohibited]

> sellers must ensure that any item they are offering for sale… is permitted by,
> and complies with, all applicable laws **in their jurisdiction, and in the
> jurisdictions of their buyers**. In addition, sellers must ensure they comply
> with applicable shipping terms and requirements in their jurisdiction
> — *Prohibited or Restricted Items policy*

Note the named jurisdictional-shipment restriction is about **fresh produce**, not
seafood. **The real interstate constraint on seafood is federal/state law, not
Whatnot policy** — shipping seafood across state lines is what pulls in FDA
seafood HACCP (21 CFR 123) and interstate commerce status. That belongs to the
licensing tickets, not this one; Whatnot simply requires it and does not enforce
the details itself.

### 5. Seller fee structure

From [Whatnot seller fees](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/4847069165965-Whatnot-seller-fees)
(updated 2026-08-11, edited that same morning — this page changes often).

Two fees per item sold:

- **Commission fee** — percentage of the item's **final sale price, excluding
  shipping and taxes**.
- **Payment processing fee** — percentage of **total order value** (item price +
  buyer-paid shipping + buyer-paid taxes), **plus a fixed transaction fee**.

For a US seller, Fresh & Specialty Food is not broken out separately and falls
under **"All other categories"**:

| Category (US/CA/AU) | Commission | Payment processing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comics, TCG, Sports Singles, Toys & Hobbies | 8% up to $1,500; 0% above | 2.9% of total order value + $0.30 |
| Coins & Money | 4% up to $1,500; 0% above | 2.9% + $0.30 |
| Pallets (Wholesale) | 4% (limited time) | 2.9% + $0.30 |
| **All other categories** (incl. Fresh & Specialty Food) | **8% on final sale price** | **2.9% of total order value + $0.30** |

Note the asymmetry that bites a perishables seller: the **8% commission excludes
shipping, but the 2.9% processing fee includes it.** With $35–$65 buyer-paid
overnight shipping on every order, the processing fee is inflated well beyond what
a card-shop seller pays on the same item price. Whatnot states this directly:

> higher shipping costs increase the total order value, which raises the processing
> fee and lowers earnings

No listing fees: "Whatnot doesn't charge fees to create, store, or manage listings."
Giveaways carry no seller fees (you still pay shipping). Seller-generated coupons
reduce the fee base; Whatnot-generated coupons do not affect seller earnings.
Taxes may apply on top of fees depending on location.

**Worked example on a plausible order** (arithmetic mine, using Whatnot's stated
formulas — not a quoted Whatnot example): $80 fish + $45 UPS Next Day Air (0–7 lb)
+ ~$0 tax = $125 total order value. Commission 8% × $80 = $6.40. Processing
2.9% × $125 + $0.30 = $3.93. Net to seller ≈ **$69.67 on an $80 fish, before cost
of goods, box, coolant, and labor.** Effective take ≈ 12.9% of the item price.

Whatnot's own quoted example, for comparison:

> Final sale price: $50 / Commission (8%): $4 / Total order value (including
> shipping and taxes): $58.70 / Payment processing fee (2.9% of $58.70 + $0.30):
> $2.00 / **Your net earnings: $50 − $4 − $2 = $44.00**

### 6. Do policies differ for a licensed food business vs. an individual seller?

**No — not in Whatnot's written policy.** Nothing in the Food & Drink Policy, the
Gated Categories article, or the Seller Verification article creates a separate
track, a separate fee schedule, or a separate application for a registered
business entity. Identity verification is of a **person** (government photo ID via
Stripe/Persona), not of an entity. The same 8% + 2.9% + $0.30 applies to everyone.

The distinction enters **indirectly**, in three ways:

1. **Compliance obligations are written to presume a licensed operator.** The
   seller-requirements list expects things only a licensed food business has:
   > Maintaining applicable inspection and certification requirements (e.g., USDA
   > inspection for meat, NSSP/ICSSL for molluscan shellfish, **HACCP plan as
   > applicable**)

   and compliance with "FDA, USDA, state health departments, cottage food laws,
   produce quarantines." Whatnot names **cottage food laws** explicitly, which
   acknowledges home-based food sellers exist — but California cottage food law
   does not cover seafood, so that path is almost certainly closed here (a
   licensing-ticket question, not a Whatnot one).

2. **Application review is discretionary** on "seller experience, compliance
   requirements and overall alignment with marketplace standards." A licensed
   wholesaler with a HACCP plan plainly presents better than an unlicensed
   individual, even though the written rules are identical.

3. **Documentation on demand.** "Sellers will provide Whatnot with applicable
   compliance documentation or evidence upon request." An individual with no
   paperwork fails that request; a licensed business passes it.

**Bottom line for the Costa Mesa question:** Whatnot will not stop a home-based
individual at the policy-text level. California law is what decides whether that
home operation is legal, and Whatnot has reserved the right to ask for proof and
pull access. The platform is not the binding constraint — **the licensing tickets
are.**

## Open questions

1. **Does "wild or hunted game meats" reach wild-caught fish?** The prohibition
   names land mammals only (venison, boar, elk) and sits under the meat/poultry
   inspection heading, and the separate Seafood clause plainly contemplates
   wild-caught product. The reading that wild-caught finfish is fine is an
   inference from document structure, **not an explicit carve-out**. Given that
   California wild-caught fish *is the entire product*, this deserves a written
   answer from Whatnot seller support before money moves. **Could not confirm from
   policy text alone.**
2. **What does the Fresh & Specialty Food application actually ask for?** The
   application form is only reachable by scheduling a show or listing a product
   in-app, so its contents could not be observed without an account taking that
   action. Whether it demands a license number, a HACCP plan, or a facility
   address up front is unknown — and it is the cheapest remaining unknown to
   resolve. **Recommend: create the account, complete verification, and start the
   application to read the form.** That costs nothing and answers it directly.
3. **Is there a separate approval bar for finfish vs. shellfish?** Policy names
   ICSSL for molluscan shellfish only. Whether Whatnot's reviewers apply an
   equivalent informal standard to finfish sellers is not documented.
4. **Does Whatnot verify a home address as the ship-from?** Nothing found on
   whether a residential origin address triggers additional review for perishables.
   Not addressed in any policy page located.
5. **Fee volatility.** The seller fees page was edited the morning of the day it
   was checked, and carries several time-limited promotional rates. The 8% "all
   other categories" rate is the standing rate, but re-check before modeling.
6. **Buyer Protection exposure.** The Buyer Protection Policy was identified but
   not read in this pass. Spoilage billbacks are explicitly a seller risk; the
   precise refund-liability mechanics are worth their own look if the effort
   proceeds.
