# CA Spiny Lobster Middleman Plan – Elliott (Long Beach / OC) — Oct 15, 2026

## Two legal paths
- **A. Buy direct from fishermen at the dock** → you are the "first receiver": need CDFW **Fish Receiver's License ($1,063)** and must file a landing receipt (fish ticket, via E-Tix) for every purchase.
- **B. Buy from a licensed receiver/wholesaler** → CDFW **Fish Wholesaler's License ($720)**. No fish tickets, but you pay a markup.
- Doing both (+processing/importing) → Multifunction license ($2,645.75). Recreationally caught lobster can **never** be bought or sold.

## Licensing checklist
1. Form the business (LLC/sole prop) and get an EIN.
2. CDFW Commercial Fish Business License: Receiver $1,063 (Path A) or Wholesaler $720 (Path B). Valid Jan 1 to Dec 31, 2026, so you must buy again for 2027 to keep operating Jan 1–Mar 17, 2027 (2027 fee UNVERIFIED). Fees include a 3% application fee capped at $7.50. Buy online or at a CDFW sales office; LRB commercial line (916) 928-5822.
3. Path A: set up E-Tix electronic landing receipts (PSMFC E-Tix; required for Receiver and Multifunction licensees). Enter each landing within 3 business days; buyer and fisherman both sign and keep copies for 4 years. Fish landing fees apply. Record each fisherman's commercial license and Lobster Permit number on every ticket.
4. CDPH Food & Drug Branch **Processed Food Registration** for seafood distributors (lobster counts as a crustacean). Fees: base fee **$524** (warehouse ≤5,000 sq ft, or 0–2 people incl. owners; CDPH 8610) + **$250 Seafood HACCP fee** + **$100 Food Safety Fee** (waived at under $20k wholesale income) = **$874**. You need a written Seafood HACCP plan (21 CFR 123), and the facility you store from gets inspected. A trained HACCP person (course ~$300–600, ESTIMATE) is the practical way to get there.
5. If you sell live to consumers from a storefront or tank: county environmental health retail food permit (LA County or OC Health Care Agency). Long Beach runs its **own** health department.
6. City business license. **Long Beach wholesale: $473.47 base + $12.27/employee (FY 7/1/25–6/30/26 table; the FY 2026-27 table that took effect July 1, 2026 could not be loaded, so the current rate is UNVERIFIED. Call (562) 570-6211)**. For OC cities (Newport Beach, Dana Point, Irvine, etc.), check that city's own rate. Many are cheaper flat fees (not verified here).
7. CDTFA seller's permit (free to register). Food products are generally exempt from sales tax (UNVERIFIED for this audit), but you still need the permit for resale and records.
8. Insurance: general and product liability, plus commercial auto.
9. LLC: LLC-1 $70, LLC-12 $20 within 90 days, FTB $800 annual tax (first due Jan 15, 2027 if formed Oct 16, 2026; 2027 tax due Apr 15, 2027).

## Key rules / gotchas
- 2026–27 commercial season: **Oct 7, 2026 to Mar 17, 2027** (23 weeks; 14 CCR 121). That's the first Wednesday in October through the first Wednesday after Mar 15. No sale or possession outside the season except lobster frozen/cooked-and-frozen during the season, so this is a 5-month business.
- Minimum size: **3¼ in carapace**. You're liable if you possess shorts.
- Lobster must be kept **whole** (measurable) until sold to the ultimate consumer. Tailing is not allowed (14 CCR 121.5(c)). Exception: licensed fish businesses/processors may split lengthwise if both halves stay banded/packaged together (121.5(d)). Petition 2026-02 to allow tailing pushed to March 2027: UNVERIFIED (secondary source only).
- Only buy from fishermen who hold a commercial license **and** a Lobster Operator Permit. Ask to see both.
- Live holding: chilled seawater tanks or wet cold storage at ~45–50°F. Dead lobster spoils fast. Plan for 3–10% shrink.
- Demand risk: the 2025 season opened at $7–10/lb fisherman-to-wholesaler (Catalina Offshore, a wholesaler, Oct 10 2025), down from $20–24/lb the year before (aquaticguide.com, secondary). Cause per Catalina: Australia resumed selling to China (China still buys, at lower prices). No official CDFW 2025 ex-vessel average was found (UNVERIFIED). Good for buying cheap, but the market could swing back.
- Watch CDFW domoic acid closures.

## Long Beach vs Orange County
- **Long Beach:** big-city license ($473+) and a city health department. Close to LA restaurants and the Asian markets that pay well for live lobster. Few retail dock fishermen, so you'd likely buy at the San Pedro / LB docks under Path A.
- **Newport Beach Dory Fleet:** fishermen already sell direct to consumers under their own Fisherman's Retail License. Good for building relationships, but they're competing with you for retail. Pitch them on buying their surplus volume.
- **Dana Point:** active lobster trap fleet. Path A works well here for supplying OC restaurants.

## Margin example (Path A, live whole lobster)
- Buy (ex-vessel) $10/lb. Source: 2025 range was $7–10, and Catalina Offshore said it paid ≥$9 (S). No 2026 figure has been published yet.
- Sell wholesale to restaurants/markets at $16/lb (E). San Diego retail ran $12–30/lb in fall 2025 (S, anecdotal).
- After 8% shrink and $0.50/lb for ice/tank/supplies, net margin is **$4.22/lb** ($16 × 0.92 − $10 − $0.50) (E). Fuel is a separate $60/week.
- Break-even = (year-1 fixed $14,563.47 + fuel $60 × 17 wk) ÷ $4.22 ≈ **3,693 lb**. Over the ~17 weeks you can realistically sell (≈Nov 16 2026–Mar 17 2027) that's about **217 lb/week**. If you could sell all 23 weeks: ≈3,778 lb, ≈164 lb/week.
- At the base case of 150 lb/week for 17 weeks, year 1 loses ≈ **$4,822** (equipment included).
